Travella is an all-in-one train schedule app built around the newly launched Lagos–Abeokuta–Ibadan train system. It brings accurate schedules, station details, real-time updates, and ticket information into one accessible place — solving a gap the official channels hadn't solved yet.

Designing Travella meant addressing the reality of first-time train travelers on a brand-new rail line, where accurate schedules, station details, and pricing were scattered across calls, hearsay, and outdated pages. The goal was to give people one dependable, user-friendly place to plan a train journey from start to finish.

Usability & Deduction

Research findings were translated directly into product decisions.

Two takeaways from the surveys shaped features that weren't part of the original brief.

  • A minority of users wanted to purchase train tickets directly, without leaving the app.
  • Several mentioned double-checking prices elsewhere even after finding a schedule, out of habit more than necessity.
Research insight ↳
  • Added a dedicated ticket pricing flow reachable in one tap from any schedule screen.
  • Placed price and timing side by side on the same view, so there's nothing left to verify elsewhere.
Design response ↳
  • Users wanted to stay updated on train status and changes, not just look it up once.
  • Several described checking multiple sources repeatedly on travel day, unsure if anything had changed since they last looked.
Research insight ↳
  • Introduced real-time notifications for departures, delays, and schedule changes, delivered in-app.
  • Paired that with a live station map, so travel-day status and directions live in the same place.

Final Design

Launching the Travella app

A captivating first impression

Onboarding introduces Travella's core value in three quick beats — real-time updates, effortless station navigation, and a single home for every schedule — before dropping the traveler straight into the app.

Train schedule, front and center

Selecting a route surfaces the next departure, weekday/weekend toggles, and a full morning-to-evening trip list — everything needed to plan a journey in one screen, without digging.

Ticket pricing, one tap away

Every schedule links straight into pricing — no separate app, no calling around. Selecting "View full schedule" or "Ticket prices" keeps both paths available from the same screen.

Real-time station navigation

A live map tracks distance and direction to the nearest station, with a clear start/exit state so travelers always know where they stand before boarding.

Notifications that actually help

Departure reminders, delay alerts, and schedule changes are pushed the moment they happen — the direct response to travelers checking multiple sources out of habit rather than need.

Feedback, made easy to give

A lightweight in-app prompt lets travelers flag issues or share ratings right after a trip, so the team can catch friction points without relying on app-store reviews.

Outcome

One dependable place for a brand-new rail line.

Travella brings train schedules, station details, real-time updates, and ticket pricing for the Lagos–Abeokuta–Ibadan line into one app — replacing calls, scattered searches, and outdated pages with a single user-friendly source, while staying flexible enough to extend to other rail systems.

Research-led

Every core feature — ticket pricing in-app, real-time notifications — traces back to a specific finding from user interviews, not an assumption.

Built to extend

Structured around two pillars — schedule and pricing — so the same system can be adapted to other Nigerian rail lines with minimal rework.

Dev-ready

Feasibility studies with developers on real-time updates and interactive maps mean the design holds up outside of prototypes, not just inside them.

Learning

The best answer to information chaos was structure, not more content.

  • Most of the friction wasn't a design problem — it was the absence of a single trustworthy source, so naming that gap precisely shaped every decision after.
  • Small features that came directly from research findings — real-time updates, in-app ticket pricing — earned their place far more than anything invented up front.
